\begin{abstract}
Employment-related text such as resumes and job postings encodes skills in unstructured language, yet downstream career services require structured, traceable skill representations for matching, gap analysis, and training recommendations.
This thesis presents SkillLLM\_v2, a context-aware graph-augmented skill intelligence system built on supervised span extraction from open-weight language models.
The work proceeds in two verified stages.
First, a controlled SkillSpan benchmark compared five locally fine-tuned open-weight models under identical data splits and evaluation code; Mistral Small 3.1 24B achieved the strongest completed exact span F1 (0.665) among feasible models on 3{,}569 test examples, while Gemma 4 26B failed repeatedly on available 2$\times$24\,GB GPU hardware.
Second, a Stage-2 system centers on an 80-epoch Mistral LoRA extractor (exact span F1 0.670 on the same benchmark), augmented by GLiNER and dictionary candidates, boundary refinement, taxonomy linking, and a non-regression wrapper gate before graph-based job matching and course recommendation.
Encoder baselines under the same evaluation protocol reached exact span F1 of 0.059 (BERT) and 0.076 (zero-shot GLiNER).
The full CAG/graph wrapper preserved anchor extraction quality while achieving taxonomy linking coverage of 0.890 on verified artifacts.
Labeled evaluation of job matching and course recommendation was not available in the repository at thesis preparation time; those components are demonstrated on synthetic demo inputs only.
\end{abstract}
